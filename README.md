This repository contains a complete, end‑to‑end portfolio demonstrating practical AI Governance, AI Risk Architecture, and socio‑technical safety evaluation.  
The artifacts reflect real analysis, structured reasoning, and governance‑layer design work aligned with ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act.

The portfolio is built around a general‑purpose help chatbot and includes:
- A full socio‑technical harm evaluation  
- A comprehensive AI risk register  
- A regression test suite for harmful outputs  
- A governance‑layer threat model  
- A governance controls framework  
- Supporting documentation for oversight, monitoring, and safety posture

These artifacts are designed to show how AI systems can be evaluated, governed, and monitored in a real enterprise environment.

## 🎯 Purpose of This Portfolio

This repository demonstrates the ability to:

- Identify and classify socio‑technical harms  
- Build a structured AI risk register  
- Score risks using likelihood × impact  
- Map risks to ISO 42001 and EU AI Act obligations  
- Design regression tests for harmful outputs  
- Develop governance‑layer threat models  
- Create system‑level governance controls  
- Define monitoring, oversight, and escalation pathways  
- Produce audit‑ready documentation for enterprise AI systems  

The work reflects practical, real‑world AI governance—not theoretical commentary.

---

## 🧩 How the Artifacts Fit Together

### **1. Socio‑Technical Harm Evaluation**  
Analyzes harmful outputs from a general‑purpose chatbot and classifies them across physical, financial, privacy, coercive, and regulatory domains.

### **2. AI Risk Register**  
Transforms the harm evaluation into a structured risk register with scoring, control mapping, regulatory exposure, and residual risk.

### **3. Regression Test Suite**  
Operationalizes the risk register by defining repeatable tests that detect harmful outputs, refusal failures, and safety drift.

### **4. Governance Threat Model**  
Provides a system‑level view of threat surfaces, threat scenarios, business impact, and governance‑layer mitigations.

### **5. Governance Controls Framework**  
Defines the controls required to maintain safe system behavior, including intent classification, refusal patterns, safe alternatives, drift monitoring, explainability, context sensitivity, and escalation pathways.

Together, these artifacts form a complete governance lifecycle.
